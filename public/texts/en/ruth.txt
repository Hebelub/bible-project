[chapter=1]
[1] Back when the judges ruled Israel, a famine hit the land. A man from Bethlehem in Judah left with his wife and two sons to live in the land of Moab for a while.  
[2] His name was Elimelech, his wife was Naomi, and his sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They settled in Moab.  
[3] But then Elimelech, Naomi’s husband, died, leaving her alone with her two sons.  
[4] The sons married two Moabite women—one named Orpah, the other Ruth. They lived there for about ten years.  
[5] Then both sons, Mahlon and Chilion, died too. Naomi was left without her husband or her children.  
[6] While in Moab, she heard that the Lord had provided for His people in Israel with food again. So she got ready to head home with her daughters-in-law.  
[7] They left the place where they’d been living and started toward the land of Judah.  
[8] On the way, Naomi said to her daughters-in-law, “Go back home to your mothers. May the Lord show you steadfast love, just as you’ve shown to the dead and to me.  
[9] May He give you new husbands and a safe home.” She kissed them goodbye, but they started crying loudly.  
[10] They said, “No, we’re coming with you to your people.”  
[11] Naomi replied, “Come on, my daughters, go home. Why stay with me? I can’t have more sons for you to marry.  
[12] Go back—I’m too old to remarry. Even if I had a husband tonight and bore sons,  
[13] would you wait for them to grow up? No, that’d be too hard for you. The Lord’s hand has been against me.”  
[14] They cried even more. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye and left, but Ruth clung to her.  
[15] Naomi said, “Look, your sister-in-law went back to her people and her gods. Do the same.”  
[16] But Ruth said, “Don’t make me leave you. Where you go, I go. Where you live, I live. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.  
[17] Where you die, I’ll die, and there I’ll be buried. May the Lord punish me severely if anything but death separates us.”  
[18] Seeing Ruth’s resolve, Naomi stopped trying to persuade her.  
[19] The two continued to Bethlehem. When they arrived, the whole town was buzzing: “Is that Naomi?”  
[20] She said, “Don’t call me Naomi anymore—call me Mara, because the Almighty has made my life bitter.  
[21] I left full of life, but the Lord brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi when the Lord has testified against me and the Almighty has brought me sorrow?”  
[22] So Naomi returned with Ruth, her Moabite daughter-in-law, from Moab. They arrived in Bethlehem just as the barley harvest began.

[chapter=2]
[1] Naomi had a relative on her husband’s side, a wealthy and influential man named Boaz, from Elimelech’s family.  
[2] Ruth, the Moabite, said to Naomi, “Let me go to the fields and pick up leftover grain behind the harvesters, if someone’s kind enough to let me.” Naomi said, “Go ahead, my daughter.”  
[3] Ruth went out and gleaned grain after the harvesters. By chance, she ended up in a field belonging to Boaz, from Elimelech’s family.  
[4] Just then, Boaz arrived from Bethlehem. He greeted the harvesters, “The Lord be with you!” They replied, “The Lord bless you!”  
[5] Boaz asked the overseer, “Who’s that woman?”  
[6] The overseer said, “She’s the Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from Moab.  
[7] She asked to glean and gather grain behind the harvesters. She’s been working since morning, with just a short break.”  
[8] Boaz said to Ruth, “Listen, my daughter, don’t go to other fields. Stay here with my women.  
[9] Watch where they harvest and follow them. I’ve told the men not to bother you. If you’re thirsty, drink from their water jars.”  
[10] Ruth bowed low and said, “Why are you so kind to me, a foreigner?”  
[11] Boaz replied, “I’ve heard all about how you’ve supported your mother-in-law since your husband died—how you left your family and homeland to come to a people you didn’t know.  
[12] May the Lord reward you for what you’ve done and give you full blessing under the wings of Israel’s God, where you’ve sought refuge.”  
[13] Ruth said, “Thank you, sir. You comfort me and speak kindly, even though I’m just a servant.”  
[14] At mealtime, Boaz said, “Come, eat some bread and dip it in vinegar.” She sat with the harvesters, and he gave her roasted grain. She ate her fill and had some left over.  
[15] When she went back to gleaning, Boaz told his men, “Let her glean among the sheaves too, and don’t scold her.  
[16] Drop some grain on purpose for her to pick up, and don’t say anything.”  
[17] Ruth gleaned until evening, gathering nearly an ephah of barley.  
[18] She carried it back to town and showed Naomi what she’d collected. She also gave her the leftovers from her meal.  
[19] Naomi asked, “Where did you work today? Who was so kind to you?” Ruth told her about the man she’d worked for. Naomi said, “Bless him in the Lord’s name!  
[20] He hasn’t stopped showing steadfast love to the living and the dead.” She added, “He’s one of our closest relatives, a kinsman-redeemer.” Ruth said, “He told me to stay with his women until the harvest is done.”  
[21] Ruth added, “Yeah, he said to stick with his workers until everything’s harvested.”  
[22] Naomi said, “Good, my daughter. Stay with his women so no one bothers you in other fields.”  
[23] Ruth stayed with Boaz’s women through the barley and wheat harvests, living with her mother-in-law.

[chapter=3]
[1] Naomi said to Ruth, “My daughter, I need to find a secure place for you where you’ll be cared for.  
[2] Boaz is our relative, and you’ve been with his women. Tonight, he’ll be winnowing barley at the threshing floor.  
[3] Wash up, put on some perfume, wear your best clothes, and go down there. Don’t show yourself until he’s done eating and drinking.  
[4] Notice where he lies down. When he’s asleep, go uncover his feet and lie there. He’ll tell you what to do.”  
[5] Ruth said, “I’ll do what you say.”  
[6] She went to the threshing floor and did exactly as her mother-in-law instructed.  
[7] Boaz ate and drank, got in a good mood, and lay down by the grain pile. Ruth quietly went, uncovered his feet, and lay down.  
[8] In the middle of the night, he woke up startled and turned over—there was a woman at his feet!  
[9] “Who are you?” he asked. “I’m Ruth, your servant,” she said. “Spread your cloak over me, for you’re my kinsman-redeemer.”  
[10] Boaz said, “May the Lord bless you, my daughter! This act of steadfast love is even greater than the first—you didn’t chase after young men, rich or poor.  
[11] Don’t worry, I’ll do what you ask. Everyone in town knows you’re a virtuous woman.  
[12] It’s true I’m a kinsman, but there’s one closer than me.  
[13] Stay here tonight. In the morning, I’ll see if he’ll redeem you. If not, I will, as the Lord lives. Rest here until morning.”  
[14] She lay at his feet until morning but got up before dawn so no one would see. Boaz thought, “No one should know a woman was here.”  
[15] He said, “Give me your shawl.” He filled it with six measures of barley and put it on her back. Then he went into town.  
[16] Ruth returned home, and Naomi asked, “How’d it go, my daughter?” Ruth told her everything.  
[17] “He gave me these six measures of barley and said, ‘Don’t go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’”  
[18] Naomi said, “Wait and see, my daughter. He won’t rest until this is settled today.”

[chapter=4]
[1] Boaz went to the town gate and sat down. Just then, the closer kinsman passed by. Boaz said, “Come over and sit, friend.” He sat down.  
[2] Boaz gathered ten of the town’s elders and said, “Sit here.” They sat.  
[3] He said to the kinsman, “Naomi, who came back from Moab, is selling the land that belonged to our brother Elimelech.  
[4] I wanted to let you know: Buy it in front of these witnesses, the elders of the people. If you’ll redeem it, do it. If not, tell me, because you’re first in line, and I’m next.” The kinsman said, “I’ll redeem it.”  
[5] Boaz said, “When you buy the land from Naomi, you also get Ruth, the Moabite, the widow of the deceased, to keep his name alive on the property.”  
[6] The kinsman said, “Then I can’t—it’d ruin my own inheritance. You redeem it; I can’t.”  
[7] Back then in Israel, to finalize a redemption or transaction, one took off a sandal and gave it to the other as a witness.  
[8] The kinsman said to Boaz, “Buy it yourself,” and took off his sandal.  
[9] Boaz said to the elders and people, “You’re witnesses today: I’m buying everything that belonged to Elimelech, Chilion, and Mahlon from Naomi.  
[10] And I’m taking Ruth, the Moabite, Mahlon’s widow, as my wife to keep the deceased’s name alive on his inheritance, so his name won’t die out among his brothers or in this town. You’re witnesses.”  
[11] Everyone said, “We’re witnesses. May the Lord make this woman who’s entering your house like Rachel and Leah, who built the house of Israel. Prosper in Ephrathah and be renowned in Bethlehem.  
[12] May your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, through the children the Lord gives you with this woman.”  
[13] Boaz took Ruth as his wife, and they lay together. The Lord let her conceive, and she bore a son.  
[14] The women said to Naomi, “Blessed be the Lord who gave you a redeemer today! May his name be renowned in Israel.  
[15] He’ll renew your spirit and care for you in your old age, because your daughter-in-law who loves you has borne him—she’s better to you than seven sons.”  
[16] Naomi took the child, held him close, and became like a mother to him.  
[17] The neighbors named him Obed, saying, “A son is born to Naomi!” Obed became the father of Jesse, who became the father of David.  
[18] This is the genealogy of Perez: Perez fathered Hezron,  
[19] Hezron fathered Ram, Ram fathered Amminadab,  
[20] Amminadab fathered Nahshon, Nahshon fathered Salmon,  
[21] Salmon fathered Boaz, Boaz fathered Obed,  
[22] Obed fathered Jesse, Jesse fathered David.
